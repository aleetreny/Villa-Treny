## Conversation progress and the failed deals

All eight additional replies target the actual latest speaker, and each uses a distinct incoming turn. The useful text often advances the discussion: B asks Cato for precise thresholds; E proposes a meeting time; Iris agrees to listen; Ferran discusses where to meet. However, **five of these eight complete decisions are rejected**, because each attaches a work deal while omitting `message.close:false`.

The same defect rejects G, L and T during the first round. All eight generated deals are zero-cell work proposals. Several are substantively wrong even if the field were repaired: B hires Cato to grow in the garden while discussing medical criteria; E attaches repair to a roster meeting; Iris attaches repair to listening to an audio file; Ferran hires Mara to dig while discussing old messages; Ama hires Ulla to record an Infirmary visit while offering fiction. Lior's repair proposal is the clearest match to the requested work, but still fails as emitted.

**Automatically adding `close:false` would be an unsafe semantic “fix”: it would turn unrelated proposals into real obligations.** The protocol and prompt need a clearer decision between ordinary dialogue and choosing a meaningful economic agreement. A zero-price cooperative task can be legitimate, but its action still has to match the negotiated purpose.

Among accepted continuations, N–O reaches four messages about water work and capacity. S–X reaches three, including an actual refusal of digging help and acceptance of quiet. P–Q also reaches three, but progresses by inventing that Quim has arrived. V–W repeats the original “watching the grid or staring at the walls” question after Wen has answered it, so a new turn does not necessarily represent new progress.
