## One world, two clocks

The existing physical engine remains authoritative for resources, needs, work, debts and real outcomes. A physical watch lasts six hours. A model response cannot advance it, mint wages or grant another action. Visual walking is an independent presentation layer; residents can speak across rooms, and pixel co-location is not required for a decision or economic interaction.

A single Durable Object alarm serves the earliest physical, cognitive or retry deadline; the hourly cron only repairs scheduling. Cognition has a minimum three-minute cadence, a six-hour individual review target and 30-minute failed-attempt backoff. Eligible residents without a successful thought are prioritized; only their first opportunity bypasses soft pacing, never quota. Thereafter each provider spaces its remaining estimated opportunities using actual recorded costs, preserving unknown charges at their maximum. Cloudflare uses UTC allowance; Groq keeps rolling expiry. The next time is anchored to a real dispatch, survives restart and is unchanged by observer reads. Pure pacing waits create no job or resident attempt. Replies still count as individual reviews; neither 480 calls/day nor full six-hour coverage is guaranteed. [The offline counterexample and regression](research/cognition-pacing-2026-09-08.md) show the improvement and its limits. Twenty-five residents do not mean 25 continuously running model streams.

Sources: [scheduler](../workers/habitat-runtime/src/society-scheduler.ts), [Durable Object](../workers/habitat-runtime/src/habitat-world.ts), [physical integration](../src/lib/habitat/society/physical.ts).

Preparation considers at most 25 candidates in scheduler order using one shared quota snapshot per provider; an oversized first context cannot block a smaller admissible one. If every eligible resident must wait, that estimate is reconsidered at the next known physical watch, individual review, backoff or open-conversation expiry, with a three-minute minimum. A deadline crossed while counting tokens still triggers reconsideration. Only the chosen job is persisted. Skipped residents retain their priority, and real dispatch still requires a fresh authoritative reservation.

## Minds, evidence and privacy

Society state uses codec 3 with required records and private retrieval extensions, separate from unchanged physical world codec 4. The strict reader migrates codec 1 with empty records and codec 1/2 with empty retrieval while preserving prior minds, records and chronology. SQL11 archives the exact preceding cognitive row; the public society DTO remains version 2 and excludes private queries, cursors, focus and pending delivery. Each mind retains a monotonic revision, attempt/success timestamps, one current project and bounded evidence. A project contains a goal, private rationale, public/private visibility and up to six physical steps. A social purpose can have zero steps and remain active. No empty plan may be marked completed. Both private model context and the observer label completed executable steps `steps_finished`, without certifying the broader prose goal. The persisted `completed` status remains readable; this clarification does not rewrite historical state.

Memory distinguishes observations, claims, commitments and interpretations. Bounded retrieval uses deterministic relevance, recency and importance rather than another inference. References must come from information actually supplied to that resident; duplicate citations do not create extra evidence. Another person's words are claims, and a valid citation does not prove that the model's interpretation is sound.

Within the four visible memories, preparation now protects the retained observation linked to the latest completed/failed project step and the resident's latest retained physical receipt when different. Remaining slots keep ranked retrieval. Body history is deduplicated only against observations actually displayed; if compaction removes a matching observation, the body event can become eligible again. A step's evidence ID is exposed only with its visible observation, and final references are rebuilt. Missing historical receipts are not invented, and protected evidence can cause declared essential overflow rather than silently disappear. Four residents in the revision-218 measurement still omitted their latest observation of another kind: this is bounded action-evidence retrieval, not complete memory. [Technical checkpoint and limits](research/grounded-receipt-selection-2026-09-08.md)


An actor sees its own knowledge, memories, feelings and purpose, selected public contacts and actual received turns. It does not receive another mind's hidden rationale, fears, memories or reciprocal relationship scores. Contact duties do not establish someone's current room. The [public projection](../src/lib/habitat/society/public.ts) explicitly selects visible project metadata, communicated turns and economic terms; it does not spread private minds or job payloads.

A closed exchange has no next speaker, but its recipient can still need its last message. `receivedClosure` supplies only the latest closing turn addressed to this actor after its last successful review; that turn's ID is valid evidence. The scheduler can consider that resident again under the existing pacing, backoff and quota guards. It neither reopens the conversation nor creates a reply obligation, proves reading, grants consent or marks a purpose completed. A successful review consumes this eligibility through the existing timestamp. See [closing-turn selection](../src/lib/habitat/society/closing-turn.ts).

The existing cognitive limits remain 24 memories per resident, six plan steps, 40 conversations of at most 16 turns, 64 legacy offers and 64 legacy agreements. Authored records have their own bounded arrays and identifiers, described below. Historical archive growth is separate from these active-state limits. ## Capability vocabulary inherited from protocol 3

Protocol 3 introduced this vocabulary. The released P7 producer retains kind/name `society_turn` with explicit output-contract version **7**; saved versions are dispatched using their original contract. [The adapter](../src/lib/habitat/society/capabilities.ts) validates its external grammar before translating choices into canonical engine intentions. Existing stored actions, terms and older job contracts remain readable; this is not a rewrite of historical actions or a new physical engine.

| New choice | Canonical action and actual effect |
| --- | --- |
| `accrue_labor_credit` | `work`: labour allocation weight, not immediate spendable cells or document writing. |
| `walk_room_and_log_visit` | `inspect`: travel/visit record, not sensor measurements or verified safety. |
| `accrue_two_labor_credits` | `charge` at Workshops: two labour credits, not an immediate private payment. |
| `log_stock_register` | `note`, available only to A: stock totals and the working-room register, not arbitrary authored documents. |
| `filter_water` | `clean` at Well: the defined water-producing recipe. |
| `clean_space` | `clean` outside Well: cleaning without that water yield. |

Other offered names remain `go`, `rest`, `sleep`, `eat`, `drink`, `wash`, `dig`, `grow`, `cook`, `repair` and `repay`, subject to their real restrictions. Generic `observe` is not offered in protocol 3; it remains supported in legacy state. Destinations are explicit: `at` combines travel and action within one watch, `go.room` is travel only, and `repay.target` names an actual creditor.

The scheduler renders existing steps in that vocabulary only when it can resolve their actual effect. A saved `clean` without a destination retains its eventual-room ambiguity as `legacyVerb` plus a reason; it is not labeled as filtering water merely because the resident is at the Well now. Unsupported legacy `observe` is annotated similarly. This affects supplied context only, preserving saved intentions, terms and history. [Context tests](../workers/habitat-runtime/test/society-schema.test.ts) cover these cases.

These physical capabilities do not write arbitrary documents, conduct consultations, reveal private facts or manufacture knowledge. P6 adds a separate authored-record operation; its text is an attributed claim, not a verified finding. A goal unsupported by physical operations should retain zero steps and advance through a concrete discussion if appropriate. Capability names clarify consequences; they do not mechanically prove that an open-text goal matches its selected steps.

The [SYSTEM instructions](../src/lib/habitat/society/instructions.ts) remain separate. USER begins with the actor's canonical name/ID and the full names of only the currently offered recipients, followed by the available context. The identity intervention changed both self framing and public recipient naming; the small historical probe does not isolate their individual effects.

For the ordinary P7 response branch inherited from P6, an absent project requires an authored purpose. If contact is available it also requires a message; an active reply addresses the actual other speaker. Later private reviews may remain silent. Contact does not require revealing a private goal. This first-contact rule is an explicit protocol choice, not evidence of spontaneous sociability. One inference generates one resident's intervention.

## Deals and consequences

P4 permits a first-contact proposal to an eligible recipient without fabricating an incoming offer. P6 retains this relative `deal`: transfer direction (`give`/`ask`), loan direction (`lend`/`borrow`), work role (`work`/`hire`), or acceptance/refusal of an exact incoming offer ID. A deal requires an actual message to its counterpart. In P4/P6, omitted `message.close` keeps the conversation open; explicit `close:true` conflicts with a deal. Earlier saved contracts retain their prior rules. The engine derives distinct parties and absolute dates; raw canonical `offer`/`respond` fields are not model inputs through this boundary.

New offers expire after two physical watches. Work prices are total payment for the stated units, including voluntary zero-price work. Dates must leave sufficient action slots; acceptance revalidates the original deadline rather than extending it. Funds, prior promises, prerequisites and resources are checked against current state. Counterproposals require fresh acceptance; identical terms from two people are not automatically a contract.

Only the actual counterpart's separate valid acceptance creates an agreement. Silence, expiry, friendly language, appraisal or claimed consent cannot do so. Work completes only from successful eligible physical actions within the existing per-watch action budget. Payment and fulfillment are atomic/idempotent. See [choice adapter](../src/lib/habitat/society/choice.ts), [economic authority](../src/lib/habitat/society/economy.ts) and [work tests](../src/lib/habitat/society/worker-credit.test.ts).

## Authored records in protocol 6

P6 extends the P4 object with one optional `record` operation. It does not use P5's experimental tuple. An explicit document-only private review can use the shared commit guards without inventing a reflection or message; first-purpose and actual incoming-reply requirements still apply. Common speech/project/appraisal changes and the document operation succeed or roll back together. See [adapter](../src/lib/habitat/society/record-choice.ts) and [domain](../src/lib/habitat/society/records.ts).

Drafts have server-issued identifiers, an exact author, title/text, source references, audience and SHA-256. The combined title/text limit is 600 UTF-8 bytes, including a 96-byte title limit, with at most four references. Newly prepared P6 context carries `records.draftTextMaxLength:504`: its schema caps title at 96 characters and body at 504, reserving title space. Saved preparations without that marker keep their original 600-character body grammar. These character limits prevent the observed ASCII 25+600 overflow but are not a complete UTF-8 byte guarantee; unchanged domain validation still rejects multi-byte or combined content above its byte limits. Revision names the author's own exact parent ID/hash; no draft or published text is overwritten. A private draft requires `publish:false`; publication needs either a named recipient or public audience. Sharing grants access, not reading, approval or endorsement. Public text cannot reveal private source IDs. The model receives at most three complete readable revisions with omission counts, relevant offers and all its continuing publication obligations; an inaccessible draft is not secretly supplied to a counterpart.

A draft can explicitly schedule publication, or its author can schedule an exact existing revision. Execution consumes the next available physical action slot, potentially the still-unused slot of the current watch. It runs after urgency checks and replaces another plan/routine action rather than adding one. Speech, drafting and sharing never count as a physical publication. A publication records the exact draft ID/hash and audience. No paper, ink, stock, measurements, medical authority or binding rule is invented by saving text.

Document commissions are separate from legacy `OfferTerms`. They bind exact draft ID/hash, author, payer, audience, total cells and deadline. Only the counterpart's independent acceptance creates a commitment. A matching revision or new proposal is not acceptance. Current funds and prior promises are revalidated, with the reservation bridge covering both older agreements and document commissions. A later qualifying publication is delivery evidence; previously published content cannot retroactively fulfill a new commission. Payment is conserved and idempotent; insufficient funds remain `payment_due`, and zero-price fulfilled cooperation is not payment. [Physical integration](../src/lib/habitat/society/record-watch.ts) and [record tests](../src/lib/habitat/society/records.test.ts) enforce this boundary.

The records working set allows 256 drafts, 256 publications, 256 shares, 64 offers, 64 agreements and 25 author publication intents. Per-actor cursors prevent duplicate operations without invalidating A merely because B changes an unrelated document. Eviction preserves active bindings and required parent/reference anchors, while SQL archives retain exact published bytes. Public projections expose only explicitly public publications and commission terms; private drafts, private publications, source grants and private parent identities are excluded. [Record schema](../src/lib/habitat/society/record-schema.ts), [types](../src/lib/habitat/society/record-types.ts) and [public projection](../src/lib/habitat/society/public-record-publication.ts) define the serializable limits.

The earlier [retrieval gap](research/grounded-receipt-selection-2026-09-08.md#limits-and-adjacent-finding) persists in saved P6 preparations: an accessible older revision can be absent from their offered operations. New P7 jobs add a private alternative with literal authorized search, three-entry snapshot-bound pages, exact selection, refresh and clear. One retained focus supplies complete original text within the existing three-document maximum at a later normal cognition. Metadata alone grants no content reference; ACL and exact hashes are checked again. A missing focus is reported unavailable, not pinned or substituted. Pending delivery survives preparation, quota denial and failed/P6 turns, and is acknowledged only by a matching successful P7 turn. Waiting for an interlocutor does not block private lookup or change that conversation; incoming speech still invalidates a stale mind revision. See the [implemented boundary and tests](research/authorized-retrieval-implementation-2026-09-09.md). This creates access to retained omitted records, not proof that retrieval caused or resolves sparse writing.

## Optional private appraisal

The [appraisal helper](../src/lib/habitat/society/appraisal.ts) exposes one optional assessment of the latest offered message from the current interlocutor. The actor chooses one of `trust`, `affection`, `admiration` or `resentment`, delta ±1, that exact turn reference and a private reason of 1–180 characters. Target and message index are derived by the engine. There is no automatic per-message reward and no arbitrary target, reciprocal feeling, desire or debt field.

Validation precedes the base response; application occurs only after the entire base response succeeds. The directed value is clamped to 0–100. A per-participant `Conversation.appraisedThrough` index is consumed even at a clamp boundary, so another job or memory eviction cannot reassess the same message. Replayed/failed jobs have no extra effect. The actor's reason is stored as an interpretation, not proof that the other person's claim is true. Public relationship scores can change through the existing observer, while the private reason and watermark are excluded from its agency projection.

Appraisal was introduced during society codec **version 1**. The current codec 3 reader retains that legacy compatibility and existing watermarks without inventing historical appraisals. An older strict binary cannot read this extension once written; rollback to that binary is unsupported. Do not strip watermarks to make it load. [Tests](../src/lib/habitat/society/appraisal.test.ts) cover identity, chronology, boundaries, replay, atomic failure, persistence and privacy. The minimal scope excludes new appraisals of already closed or expired conversations.

## Context and provider reservations

SQL10 routing was introduced in `13ba0a43` and is retained by the current SQL11/P7 release. Dated measurements below retain their earlier schemas, caps and model permissions; the current routing policy is described separately from those observations.

The raw core prompt now targets **6,500 UTF-8 bytes**, replacing the earlier runtime 4,200-byte target. The scheduler removes duplicate schema enumerations and empty sections, renders stored actions/recipes using capability names where resolvable, and adds explicit identity. It preserves actual evidence and records omissions; it does not fabricate shorter continuity. Final USER above 6,500 bytes is flagged as overflow rather than silently discarded. New jobs use a **1,024-token output maximum**. Neither value is a full-request provider token guarantee.

The closed provider chain for newly marked P7 jobs and saved marked P6 jobs is configured Workers AI, then eligible Groq GPT-OSS 120B and GPT-OSS 20B; unmarked jobs retain the existing CF → Groq20B route. Unavailable cognition leaves plans/routines operating. Current Workers AI configuration selects GPT-OSS 120B. Gemma 4 26B A4B and Qwen3 30B A3B remain supported Workers AI choices; GLM and Granite are not production options. No paid fallback, new account or allowance multiplication is enabled. Local oMLX supplied isolated evaluations on existing hardware and was stopped afterward; it is not a deployed provider or a dependency of cloud continuity.

Dispatch control is rechecked after asynchronous counting, before reservation. Current deployed caps are 10,000 Workers AI neurons per UTC day within the verified Workers Free allowance; each Groq model has 30 requests/8,000 tokens per rolling minute and 1,000 requests/200,000 tokens per conservative rolling 24 hours. ### Deployed SQL10 route with separate Groq model budgets

The parser defaults this flag to false. The deployed configuration selects true, and the separately recorded account readback confirms the provider permission. Each Groq model has its own 30-request/8,000-token rolling minute and 1,000-request/200,000-token conservative rolling day; a smaller configured daily ceiling is honored. The existing 32k combined/24k component byte bounds, provisional 128-token framing allowance and 1,024-token output cap remain intact. The Durable Object claims a 120-second lease and gives routing the earlier of lease expiry or prepared-turn expiry, minus 10 seconds for persistence. ## Transaction, recovery and operational limits

Job IDs include resident, mind revision and sequence. A different global publication does not alone invalidate another resident's thought; application rechecks its mind/conversation, expiry, control generation and current resources. A received result is persisted before effects, then applied once in a synchronous transaction. Pause, expired leases and delayed responses do not authorize another dispatch or duplicate effects. Retry feedback goes into USER and is included in its reservation. The released [structural feedback helper](research/structural-retry-feedback-2026-09-09.md) uses only bound, complete retained P6/P7 schema diagnostics and emits at most four issues / 600 UTF-8 bytes of known field paths, fixed codes and limits. It neither copies private candidate text nor repairs output, refunds usage or creates an extra retry. Domain or semantic failures do not receive invented structural explanations.

Society and physical state publish atomically under a new observer revision. Thinking may update coverage and personal relationships without advancing the physical clock. Terminal input compaction and terminal status commit together; exact output and identifiers are retained while duplicated prompt text becomes hashed metadata. Those hashes verify separately retained input, not reconstruct it.

[Recovery](recovery.md) captures coherent mutable rows, then pages append-only history to frozen high-water marks. [Checkpoint validation](../workers/habitat-runtime/src/checkpoint.ts) checks state, identities, revisions and hashes; migration preserves the existing world rather than seeding a replacement. Keep Worker `aleetreny-habitat-runtime`, class `HabitatWorld`, binding `HABITAT_WORLD` and habitat `habitat-canonical`. Preserve a complete private backup outside the repository; an observer snapshot is insufficient.

SQL9 adds `society_layout_backups`, preserving the previous cognitive singleton bytes/hash before codec 2 initialization, and `authored_publications`, archiving exact publication/draft pairs transactionally. Older SQL8 backups are verified against their own table set, without requiring future tables. Archive pagination uses a public allowlist; a complete private recovery includes private publications too. The [SQL9 continuity proof](research/release-2026-09-08/sql9-migration-continuity.json) verifies these permitted migration changes against two complete cuts; Worker identity is established separately by the release verification.

A separate private `provider_rejections` log retains failed candidates before parsing/normalization, the issued schema, selected-field/stage metadata and actual prompt hashes. The primary failure is stored before fallback. Retention is at most 64 rows, 16 MiB and seven days, collected on subsequent provider writes; candidates are bounded to 64 KiB and schemas to 192 KiB. Oversized candidates are marked incomplete, and full hashes are unavailable beyond the defensive 4 MiB hashing bound. Prompt text, arbitrary provider errors and HTTP headers are not captured. Diagnostic failure cannot change acceptance, refund quota or authorize a retry. The observer never exposes this log. Captured bytes and hashes do not by themselves reconstruct omitted prompts or the domain state needed for a complete replay; private backups retain diagnostic evidence independently of its live expiry. See [recovery](recovery.md) and [rejection log](../workers/habitat-runtime/src/rejection-log.ts).

The historical protocol 3 hosting dry run produced a **4,247.35 KiB** Worker upload (1,684.80 KiB compressed). The P6 release dry upload is 4,365.80 KiB / 1,711.46 KiB gzip, recorded separately in its [release verification](research/release-2026-09-08/records-release-verification.json). The earlier [local tokenizer report](research/groq-full-tokenizer-2026-09-08.md) preserves its own 4,233.98 KiB checkpoint. Current [Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/) are 64 MiB uncompressed script, no separate compressed-size limit, 128 MB combined JS/WASM memory and one second startup CPU. A [Durable Object invocation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/) defaults to 30 seconds CPU; ordinary Free Worker HTTP requests allow 10 milliseconds. Local timings/dry-run success are not production CPU measurements or a memory guarantee.

The read-only observer shows projects, actual message turns, offers, agreements, economy, explicitly public authored writings and distinct physical/cognitive health. It never schedules thought. Public polling and growing history still consume finite request, row and storage allowances. No unlimited free service or endless retained context is promised.

The same Worker packages the observer through Static Assets without a second world or unused `ASSETS` binding. Files and SPA navigation bypass the application handler; `/v1`, `/v1/*`, `/health` and `/health/*` always reach it. The separate [hosting test](../workers/habitat-runtime/test-hosting.mjs) verifies HTML/JS/PNG, API navigation, JSON errors and authenticated recovery using the real dry-run bundle, disposable SQLite and blocked outbound traffic.
