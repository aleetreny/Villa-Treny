# Genesis: the roster and the weave

The twenty-five residents, and the network of acquaintance they carried aboard.

**This file is prompt-safe.** It states only what is observable inside the world:
who these people are, where they are from, and who already knew whom.

It does not explain why they are here, and **it must never be extended to**, because
there is nothing to extend it with. No document anywhere holds the reason twenty-five
strangers were on that ship. The engine does not hold one, the author does not hold
one, and no model may ever be handed one. A theory can be falsified by evidence and
never confirmed by it.

One rule when serving this to a model: **a latent bond goes only into the prompt of
the resident who holds it.** Putting it in the other party's context hands them a
fact they do not have.

## 1. The roster

Ages are at the crash. Every resident has a distinct initial, so a graph node or a
matrix axis can be labelled with one character and stay unambiguous — an authoring
convenience with no meaning in the world.

The **assigned duty** is what the ship's triage gave each of them from a thin file
after the wreck. Several of these are plainly wrong. That is the point: the roles
are a machine's guess, they were bad on day one, and they are designed to erode
fast.

## 2. The five clusters

The prior acquaintance is not spread evenly. It falls into five dense groups with
very few edges between them.

| Cluster | Where it comes from | Members | Age range |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **I** | Kilbeg, a coastal town | Sten, Xan, Osvald, Ulla, Ama | 57–78 |
| **II** | A shipyard | Halim, Gita, Quim, Dima, Wen | 49–63 |
| **III** | One household and the people around it | Ferran, Tomás, Mara, Iris, Vero | 50–60 |
| **IV** | One street in one city | Pilar, Lior, Kes, Noor, Juno | 39–66 |
| **V** | Professional, recent, none of them close | Edda, Cato, Yara, Reva, Bex | 33–56 |

This structure is the anomaly. Twenty-five passengers drawn at random from a large
population should share almost no prior acquaintance at all, and what little they
shared should be evenly scattered. This is neither. It is five islands.

**Nobody in the habitat has noticed yet.** Each resident knows their own handful of
bonds and has no view of the whole. Only somebody who collects all twenty-five
accounts and lays them side by side can see the shape — which is exactly the sort of
slow social investigation the engine simulates, and exactly what the observer can
see on day one and they cannot.

## 4. The real bonds

Twenty-three, written by hand. Direction matters: these are six directed axes, so
where the two sides feel differently, that is stated.

### Within Cluster I — Kilbeg

1. **Ulla ↔ Osvald.** Half-siblings. Nineteen years without a word, over a house
   neither of them ended up with. Neither knew the other had booked. They have been
   civil for a hundred days and it is costing them both.
2. **Sten ↔ Xan.** Sten taught Xan to swim, forty-eight years ago. Xan is sixty and
   still defers to him. Sten finds it embarrassing and does not say so.
3. **Ama ↔ Ulla.** Schoolmates. Fond, thin, entirely uncomplicated — the only bond
   in Cluster I with nothing wrong with it.
4. **Xan ↔ Ama.** Same street. He remembers her family better than she remembers his.
5. **Sten ↔ Osvald.** Sten knew Osvald's father off the harbour. Osvald treats him
   with a respect he has never extended to anybody else, and cannot explain it.

### Within Cluster II — the yard

6. **Halim → Quim.** Halim blocked Quim's promotion twice and believes he was
   right. Quim knows it, and has arranged his whole professional self-image
   around it.
7. **Halim ↔ Gita.** Twenty years of working well together with no warmth
   whatsoever. Each would trust the other with their life and neither would sit with
   them.
8. **Gita → Quim.** Gita was hard on him because he was good. Quim resents her
   for it and does not know the reason was admiration.
9. **Dima ↔ Halim.** Dima reported a structural fault; Halim buried it; the fault was
   real; nobody was hurt, which is luck and both of them know it. **Neither has
   mentioned it in a hundred days.** It is the loudest silence in the habitat.
10. **Quim ↔ Wen.** Two overlapping years, easy, superficial, genuinely warm. The
    most functional working relationship in Cluster II by a distance.

### Within Cluster III — the household

11. **Mara ↔ Tomás.** Thirty years. The closest bond that came aboard.
12. **Tomás → Vero.** Her uncle. He took his sister's side in a rupture Vero
    refuses to have sides about. She loves him and will not discuss it.
13. **Mara → Vero.** Mara has known her since she was nine and mothers her.
    Vero finds it suffocating and cannot say so without cruelty.
14. **Ferran ↔ Mara.** He carried words between people for years and everyone knew
    it. She likes him and does not trust him, in that order.
15. **Ferran ↔ Iris.** He brought her into that circle, which is the origin of
    everything that went wrong there. Faded now into something almost fond.
16. **Mara → Iris.** Blame, twenty-two years old and undimmed. Iris knows exactly
    what she is blamed for and has decided to absorb it.

### Within Cluster IV — the street

17. **Pilar ↔ Kes.** Same building, nine years. Warm, daily, shallow, and it has
    survived the crash better than any of the deep bonds.
18. **Kes ↔ Juno.** He rode her route. Nodding acquaintance that has turned, in a
    hundred days, into something neither expected.
19. **Lior → Pilar.** Lior owed the bakery money and is embarrassed. Pilar wrote
    it off years ago and cannot understand why he is odd with her.

### Within Cluster V — recent and professional

20. **Cato ↔ Yara.** He was briefly her clinician. Neither has raised it. It makes
    every conversation between them slightly wrong.

### The bridges

Only four edges cross clusters. They are the reason the graph is a graph and not
five separate ones, and they are the seams an investigator would pull at.

21. **Cato (V) ↔ Noor (IV).** Two doctors who met at conferences. Weak, cordial,
    and now the axis on which the habitat's entire medical politics turns. They
    come from different parts of the structure, which is why neither can place
    the other.
22. **Ferran (III) ↔ Halim (II).** Ferran drew for the yard for one season,
    twenty-odd years ago, and neither of them thought about it again until they
    recognised each other across the Common on the fourth day. Halim has since
    remembered that the drawings were good and that he never said so.
23. **Pilar (IV) ↔ Ama (I).** Same region originally. They recognised the accent in
    the first week. **This bond formed after the crash** — the first new real bond in
    the habitat, and the proof that the weave grows.
24. **Noor (IV) ↔ Vero (III).** See below: this one is latent.

## 5. The latent bonds

Six. In each, one resident knows something about the other that the other does not
know they know. Drawn in the weave as a dotted edge: the observer can see there is
something and cannot read what.

Every one carries a **route to surfacing**. A latent bond with no route is a dotted
edge that never resolves, and would be a cheat.

---

**L1 — Edda → Bex.** *Edda knows.*

Bex sold Edda her ticket. Edda remembers the desk, the office and the conversation.
Bex sold thousands of fares that year and has no memory of her at all.

Edda has not mentioned it, for the ordinary reason that the longer you do not mention
something the stranger it becomes to mention it. A hundred days in, saying *you sold
me my ticket* would now require explaining why she did not say it on the first day.

*Route:* anything that reaches the booking office, the fares, or how each of them came
to be here; Bex recognising her unprompted; Edda deciding the weight of not-saying has
overtaken the weight of saying.

---

**L2 — Gita → Lior.** *Gita knows.*

Gita was the anonymous lender behind a debt Lior never repaid. He never learned who
had lent it. She has known his face since the first week and has said nothing,
partly out of tact and mostly because she enjoys it.

*Route:* Gita chooses to say it; or a debt dispute inside the habitat pushes her into
saying it to make a point about character.

---

**L3 — Juno → Reva.** *Juno knows.*

Juno was in a transit station some years ago and watched Reva sit with a crying older
woman for forty minutes and then walk away without giving her name. It is the kindest
thing Juno has ever seen a stranger do and she has never forgotten the face. Reva has
no memory of the afternoon at all.

*Route:* Juno mentions it — most likely as a compliment, at a bad moment, meaning
well.

> Engine note: Juno has been holding a small piece of somebody else's life for years
> and has no idea it is worth anything. Whether it turns out to be worth anything is
> not decided in advance, here or anywhere.

---

**L4 — Noor → Vero.** *Noor knows.*

Noor treated Vero's mother in her final year and was there at the end. Vero has no
idea; she was not the one dealing with the doctors.

*Route:* any conversation that reaches Vero's mother; a death in the habitat that
puts Noor back in that room; Noor breaking under it.

---

**L5 — Wen → Dima.** *Wen knows.*

Wen saw Dima falsify a safety log at the yard and never reported it. Dima does not
know he was seen. Wen has watched Dima hold the key to the suits for a hundred days.

*Route:* a safety failure in the habitat that echoes it; Dima's control of the dock
becoming contested; Wen deciding that keeping quiet twice is different from keeping
quiet once.

---

**L6 — Sten → Ama.** *Sten knows.*

Ama's father drowned off Kilbeg when she was four. Sten was the lifeguard who could
not reach him. It is the defining shame of a seventy-eight-year-old life. Ama does
not know he was there, and has been perfectly warm to him for a hundred days.

*Route:* anything about the sea, Kilbeg, or drowning; Sten near death; Ama asking
about her father, which she does perhaps once a year.

---

## 6. The anomaly, measured

The design brief requires that the clustering be a detectable fact rather than a
claim in the lore. It was measured against twenty thousand Erdős–Rényi graphs of the
same order and size — twenty-five nodes, twenty-nine edges, density 0.097 — using
the authored five-way partition.

| Statistic | Observed | Random mean | p |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Edges falling inside a cluster | **23** | 4.85 | < 0.00005 |
| Modularity of the five-group partition | **0.580** | −0.045 | < 0.00005 |
| Transitivity | **0.750** | 0.265 | 0.008 |

Twenty-three of the twenty-nine prior bonds fall inside a cluster where chance would
put fewer than five. The structure is not marginal and it is not a matter of
interpretation.

**And it has no explanation.** Not one withheld from the residents — one that does not
exist. The clustering is a brute fact of the world with nothing behind it, and no
investigation will ever produce a cause. This is the point rather than a gap: an
anomaly that resolves is a puzzle, and an anomaly that does not is a mystery.

Which means the barrier to discovery is **not statistical, it is social.** Any
resident who assembled all twenty-five accounts would see it immediately. Nobody has
assembled them, nobody has a reason to, and the only person with a view of the whole
is outside the world looking in.

This measurement is `src/lib/habitat/weave.test.ts`, run against the data in
`src/lib/habitat/weave.ts` on every `pnpm test`, so editing a bond cannot quietly
destroy the clue.

## 7. What the observer can see that they cannot

On day one, with no simulation having run at all, the weave already shows:

- five dense components with four bridges;
- six dotted edges whose content is unreadable;
- and, sorting the nodes by age, **the components fall into an order.**

That last one is the coldest. The clusters line up chronologically and nobody can say
why. It is the sort of regularity that looks like intent, and looking like intent is
not the same as being it.

None of the twenty-five can see any of this. Each of them knows their own handful of
bonds and nothing else. The gap between what the instrument shows and what the people
inside it know is the whole reason the instrument exists.
